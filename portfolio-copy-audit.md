# Portfolio Copy Audit and Rewrites

marthaasselin.com · July 2026

Change list organized by page. "Now" is the current live copy, "Change to" is the rewrite. Anything not listed stays as is.

Read each rewrite with one question: does this sentence tell a reader something they didn't know, or does it just sound like it means something? Flag anything that fails.

---

## What we're fixing

1. **Two-beat pitch headlines.** Setup plus clipped punchline. The most repeated tell on the site.  
2. **Fragment triples.** "Not a tour of everything. Not a redesign of the account page. A guide to the actions that determined whether someone stayed."  
3. **Aphorism closers.** Portable wisdom lines that say nothing about the project. "SEO and authenticity are not at odds when the research is right."  
4. **Vague weight.** Gesturing at significance without naming the consequence. "Costs something real."  
5. **Passive voice** where you did the thing.  
6. **Compression.** Clipped sentences that don't sound like you. Your writing is unhurried and carries the thinking.  
7. **Italicized single words in headlines** (*potential*, *heart*, *precision*). CSS change, belongs on the redesign branch punch list rather than a copy paste. Same for the bold mid-paragraph emphasis in Ourself Health.

Metrics, quotes, project facts, and the "Vibe coded with Claude" footer are untouched throughout.

**Hero headline:** unchanged per your redesign decision.

**Hero subhead** Now: "I approach design by understanding how people think, what they need, and where they get stuck. My work spans research, interaction design, and implementation, creating products that are useful, accessible, and intuitive."

Change to: "I'm a UX designer and consultant with a background in psychology and user research, which means I start with people before I start solving problems. I lead projects from discovery through delivery, and I keep the work coherent across stakeholders all the way to launch. AI runs through my workflow for research, synthesis, competitive analysis, and design validation."

Why: the original restates the header word for word.

**How I Work cards** (all four are template copy right now)

User Research Change to: "Interviews, surveys, usability testing, card sorting. The method depends on the project, but the point is always to find out what people actually do rather than what they say they do."

UX & Interaction Design Change to: "Information architecture, user flows, wireframes, and prototypes. I design so people can find their way through complex systems without much effort."

AI-Assisted Workflow Change to: "I use Claude and other AI tools for research synthesis, content development, and design exploration. On the Polly Banerjee project, an 8-site competitive audit that would have taken days took a few hours."

**Outside Work** Now: "You'll find me hiking a trail, tending to my garden, or pairing the perfect wine with a favorite dish. I believe creativity flows best when we stay connected to people, to nature, and to meaningful problems worth solving."

Change to: "You'll find me hiking, tending my garden, or cooking dinner and picking the wine to go with it."

Why: the second sentence is an aphorism with a triple inside it.

**Availability** Now: "Looking for full-time, contract, and consulting work. I do best where design has a real seat at the table: where research informs decisions, where the designer is in the room when it matters, and where the goal is something worth building."

Why: cut the second sentence entirely. It has a "where... where... where..." triple and "seat at the table" business-speak, and it's preference language a recruiter can't act on. The first sentence carries the useful information.

Optional: "before I learned what a wireframe was" is a little performy but charming and specific. I'd keep it.

## Mangala Shri Bhuti case study

**Headlines**

- "Members knew what they needed. They just couldn't find it" → "A site everyone used and no one could navigate"  
- "53 members told me where things were breaking down" → "What the member survey showed"  
- "Three categories drove most visits. None were easy to find" → "Most visits came down to three things"  
- "Turning Research Into Design Decisions" → "How the findings shaped the design"  
- "Surface what members need most" → "A homepage built around frequent tasks"  
- "Organize content around member needs" → "Seven categories instead of 35 pages"  
- "Warmth and belonging" → "Making it feel like the community"  
- "Working within what existed" → keep  
- "Wireframes, feedback, and one meaningful pivot" → "How the design came together"  
- "The first pass was too crowded" → "Version 1: too crowded"  
- "Splitting it made the difference" → "Version 2: split into Courses and Practice Resources"  
- "Research-Informed Redesign Delivered" → "What shipped"  
- "What I learned along the way" → "What I learned"

**Design Strategy opening** Change to: "The survey made the pattern hard to miss. Members weren't asking for more content, they wanted faster access to what they already used. So I reorganized 35 pages into seven categories built around how members look for things: Practice Resources, Study, Community, Archive, Programs, Seva, and News."

Then delete the duplicate. The "rather than the site's existing organizational structure" sentence in the Mega Menu card makes this same point a third time.

**Active voice fixes**

- "35 pages were reorganized" → "I reorganized 35 pages"  
- "The homepage was redesigned around" → "I redesigned the homepage around"  
- "Claude was used to accelerate synthesis" → "I used Claude to synthesize the open-ended responses"

**AI-Assisted Research Synthesis callout** Change to one sentence: "I used Claude to synthesize the open-ended survey responses, which freed me up for the information architecture and design decisions." Or cut the callout and let the Reflection section carry it.

**The revision passage** Now: "It was too much. Stakeholders flagged it and they were right. I revised without hesitation. Revising without resistance built trust that carried through the rest of the project."

Change to: "Stakeholders flagged the homepage as too crowded, and they were right. I went back and simplified it. I think being straightforward about that revision made the rest of the project go more smoothly."

**Other phrases**

- "statistically useful signal" → "enough responses to see clear patterns"  
- "Members cited navigation, search failures, and buried content" → "Members cited navigation and search failures. A lot of content was just buried."
